# Naming Research: Autonomous Construction Equipment Startup

*Research date: March 2026*

---

## What Makes a Great Name in This Space

The ideal name for an autonomous construction equipment company must do two jobs simultaneously: sound credible to a conservative infrastructure buyer (GC superintendent, mine operator, equipment fleet manager) and sound fundable to a deep-tech investor. Most startup naming optimizes for one audience. This category requires both.

**Hard requirements:**
- 1–3 syllables, pronounceable on first read
- Defensible .com or .ai domain
- Trademarkable (invented word or in non-competing context)
- Does not contain "bot," "robo," "auto," or "AI" as a prefix — too generic
- Does not sound like a contractor services company ("TerraWork," "GradeMax")
- No existing construction equipment manufacturer conflicts

**Soft requirements:**
- Sounds like it belongs on the side of a bulldozer *and* on a Y Combinator stage
- Construction vocabulary resonance (grade, terrain, earth, strata, bearing, ground)
- Hard consonants or strong vowel anchors — sounds authoritative
- Works in both engineering contexts ("deploy Gradiant on the site") and investor contexts ("leading round in Gradiant")

## How Competitors Named Themselves

### Startups in the Space

**Built Robotics** — deceptively simple. A past-tense verb turned brand. Grounded, literal, unpretentious. Says "we are of the construction world." Works with conservative buyers because it sounds like one of them. Weakness: generic, could be any contractor. Domain: builtrobotics.com (they own the .com for their brand but operate at subdomain level — common startup pattern when you need the industry word but the 1-word .com is out of reach).

**SafeAI** — compound name leading with the industry's #1 concern (safety), appended with the technology vector. Instant comprehension, low distinctiveness. Creates a ceiling: always the "safe" company, not the performance or intelligence leader.

**Teleo** — the most sophisticated name in the space. From Greek *telos* (τέλος): "end, goal, purpose, completion." Also echoes *tele-* (remote operation), directly relevant to their supervised-autonomy product. Dual resonance: "to complete" + "remote." Abstract enough to not be limiting, grounded enough in relevant etymology to reward deeper reading.

**Bedrock Robotics** — strong literal metaphor: the bedrock is what you build on, what's stable, what doesn't move. Signals foundational, dependable. "Robotics" as a suffix dates quickly but is currently standard. Domain: bedrockrobotics.com.

**Exodigo** — purpose-built invented word (Exo + digo/dico). Works specifically for subsurface mapping. Not transferable to other missions.

### Legacy OEM Names

**Caterpillar** — nickname for track system. Accident of history now a brand asset. No useful lesson for startups.

**Komatsu** — founder's family name (Takeuchi Komatsu). City of origin (Komatsu, Japan). Opaque to Western buyers but carries 100 years of equity.

**Volvo** — from Latin *volvere*, "to roll." One of the best legacy names: describes core motion, dignified, still sounds modern. The lesson: Latin/Greek roots age extremely well.

**John Deere** — founder's name, accidentally perfect for agriculture because "Deere" phonetically evokes the natural world. Can't recreate this deliberately.

### Naming Patterns in Autonomous Vehicle/Robotics Space

| Pattern | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Aspirational single word | Aurora, Kodiak, Nuro, Waymo | Premium feel, memorable, scale-ready |
| Greek/Latin root, invented | Teleo, Exodigo, Vectara | Distinctive, trademarkable, rewards reading |
| Abstract constructed | Zoox, Waabi | Short, ownable, no geographic or descriptive baggage |
| Functional descriptor | Built Robotics, SafeAI, Clearpath | Easy comprehension, lower distinctiveness |
| Environmental metaphor | Bedrock, Terra (various) | Grounded, earthy, construction-resonant |

## "Neural Automation" — Research Findings

### What Exists

**neuralautomation.io** — Active WordPress site. LinkedIn shows a company called "Neural Automation" in Manufacturing, 2–10 employees, tagline "Connecting people with automation." An active, if small, business in automation/manufacturing — not construction-specific.

**neuralautomation.com** — Domain registered; resolves to an ad-injection/monetization script, suggesting a parked domain that is not in active commercial use but is owned by someone.

**neural-automation.com** — DNS does not resolve (ENOTFOUND), suggesting unregistered or pointed nowhere.

### Brand Assessment

Even if the name were free, "Neural Automation" carries structural weaknesses for this company:

1. **Describes the technology, not the outcome.** Construction buyers don't care about neural architectures; they care about moving earth and not paying $39.69/hour for an operator.
2. **"Automation" is completely generic.** Used by thousands of companies across manufacturing, RPA software, and industrial services. It creates category confusion with enterprise IT vendors.
3. **Sounds like an enterprise IT vendor**, not a company deploying $750,000 machines on job sites.
4. **Low trademark strength.** Both words are generic in the AI/robotics space; the combination is highly descriptive of AI-driven automation — exactly the goods it would cover. Obtaining strong trademark protection would be difficult and expensive to enforce.
5. **Active company already uses this name.** (neuralautomation.io) — creating confusion risk even if no direct trademark conflict.

**Verdict: Do not use "Neural Automation" for this venture.** The name is in active use, the .com is taken, trademark protection would be weak, and it positions the company in the wrong category.

## 10 Name Proposals

### #1 — GRAVITAS ⭐ *Top recommendation*

**Pronunciation:** GRAV-ih-tass

**Meaning:** Latin *gravitas* — weight, seriousness, dignity, substance. In Roman culture, *gravitas* was the highest virtue for a person in authority. In physics, directly related to gravity and mass.

**Why it works for this space:**
- Nothing else on this list commands the same authority in a room full of infrastructure buyers
- Directly physical: the company works with machines that move mass under the force of gravity
- For conservative construction buyers skeptical of AI promises, "Gravitas" says: this is not a pilot program or a science experiment
- For investors: signals a founder who knows what they're doing and doesn't need to over-explain it
- Completely unusual for a tech company — works in its favor, creates instant distinctiveness

**Domain:** gravitas.com appears dormant/available or minimally used (check WHOIS). gravitas.ai — likely available.

**Trademark risk:** MEDIUM. Common Latin word, moderate use in business naming in different sectors. In Class 7 (construction/industrial machines) and Class 42 (AI software), likely clear. Run full clearance search.

---

### #2 — GRADIANT ⭐ *Strong construction-technical name*

**Pronunciation:** GRAY-dee-ant

**Meaning:** Blend of *grade* (fundamental construction concept — the elevation change of terrain, the core operation of every grader and dozer) + *gradient* (rate of slope — used constantly in civil engineering) + *-ant* suffix common in technical names.

**Why it works:**
- Grade is one of the most fundamental operations in heavy construction: grading land is the first step on any site
- A gradient is the mathematical term for slope — the exact parameter that motor graders and dozers manage
- Technical without being obscure — every civil engineer knows this word
- Modern feel — could be a software company, an equipment company, or a tech-hardware hybrid
- The distinctive spelling (Gradiant vs. Gradient) makes it trademarkable

**Domain:** gradiant.com appears to route to a redirect loop — likely available or minimally used. gradiant.ai — likely available.

**Trademark risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. "Gradient" is a common English word but "Gradiant" as a specific spelling is likely trademarkable in construction/robotics. Check for software/AI companies using this exact spelling.

---

### #3 — TERROVA

**Pronunciation:** teh-ROH-vah

**Meaning:** *Terra* (Latin: earth) + *nova* (Latin: new). "New earth" — evoking transformation of terrain. Phonetically echoes "innovate."

**Why it works:**
- Construction buyers will connect *terra* to their work intuitively
- "New earth" is a genuinely evocative brand promise for a company transforming how earth is moved
- 3 syllables but flows naturally; shortens cleanly to "Terrova" in casual use
- Aspirational without being vague — the company's mission is literally to create a new paradigm for how earth is managed

**Domain:** terrova.ai — appears available (DNS ENOTFOUND). terrova.com — check WHOIS separately.

**Trademark risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. "Terra" compounds are common in tech but "Terrova" as a specific coined word appears clear in construction/robotics.

---

### #4 — ARCUM

**Pronunciation:** AR-kum

**Meaning:** From Latin *arcus* (arc, curve, arch) — the engineering concept of the load-bearing arc, fundamental to construction. *Arcum* is the accusative form of *arcus* — "the arc" or "toward the arc."

**Why it works:**
- The arch is a symbol of engineering mastery (Roman aqueducts, bridges, vaults)
- Implies precision, mathematical control, and structural integrity — what you want in autonomous systems
- Unusual enough in English to be trademarkable; no obvious direct competitors
- Clean, short, professional — reads like it could be a $10B industrial software company

**Domain:** arcum.com and arcum.ai — likely available (no major company found at this domain). Verify with WHOIS.

**Trademark risk:** LOW. Novel enough as an English word to be trademarkable. Follows the tradition of Latin-derived tech names (Qualcomm, Accenture).

---

### #5 — STRATUM

**Pronunciation:** STRAY-tum

**Meaning:** Latin *stratum* — "layer, bed, pavement." In geology and construction, strata refers to the horizontal layers of earth being cut, moved, or built upon.

**Why it works:**
- Every construction geotechnical report contains the word "strata" — construction buyers know it
- Conveys precision (layers are measured and specified), depth (working with what's underground), and materiality
- For investors: sounds clean, modern, and authoritative — not gimmicky
- The company works at the stratum — the layer where real work happens

**Domain:** stratum.ai — likely available or minimally used. stratum.com — likely taken by legacy financial/data uses. Operate at stratum.ai.

**Trademark risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. Common English/Latin word, but in Class 7 (construction machinery) and Class 42 (AI software for construction), likely available. Run full clearance.

---

### #6 — COREXA

**Pronunciation:** KOR-ek-sah

**Meaning:** *Cor-* from Latin *cor/cordis* (core, heart, center) + *-exa* (precision/measurement suffix; echoes apex, hexa). "The core intelligence that drives everything."

**Why it works:**
- "Core" resonates in both construction (core drilling, core sampling, structural core) and computing (processor cores, core AI)
- The compound feels purposeful without being literal
- Reads well on equipment badges, investor decks, and in conversation
- Invented portmanteau — clean trademark

**Domain:** corexa.com and corexa.ai — both likely available. Verify with WHOIS.

**Trademark risk:** LOW. Invented portmanteau, no obvious conflicts found.

### #7 — GRADEX

**Pronunciation:** GRAY-deks

**Meaning:** *Grade* (core construction concept) + *-ex* (Latin: out of, from; also implies excellence; common in industrial brands: Apex, Vortex, Kodex).

**Why it works:**
- Most viscerally construction-sounding name on this list
- Could already be on a Caterpillar product badge — that is an asset for buyer trust
- Hard consonants signal authority and precision
- Compact, memorable, clear

**Domain:** gradex.ai — appears available. gradex.com — a Czech company (Gradex s.r.o.) uses this domain for industrial purposes.

**Trademark risk:** MEDIUM. The Czech company exists in an industrial context in the EU. In the US, the mark is likely available for construction robotics/AI, but conduct a full clearance search before investing in brand development.

---

### #8 — VOLANT

**Pronunciation:** VOH-lant

**Meaning:** From Latin *volare* (to fly) and French *volant* (flying, nimble, in motion). In heraldry, *volant* describes an eagle in full flight. In engineering, a flywheel is sometimes called a *volant wheel* due to its rotational inertia.

**Why it works:**
- Counterintuitive and memorable: the construction industry is characterized by heaviness and slowness — a name suggesting precise, fluid motion creates useful contrast
- Signals that autonomous operation changes the physics of how sites work: faster, smoother, less brutal
- The flywheel connection is also apt: a flywheel stores and releases energy efficiently — like a fleet of autonomous machines running continuously vs. idle during operator shift changes
- Stands out from other names in this space precisely because it is unexpected

**Domain:** volant.ai — likely available. volant.com — check WHOIS.

**Trademark risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. Used by some small businesses and in heraldry, but in Class 7 and Class 42, appears clear.

---

### #9 — TERREX (DO NOT USE)

**Pronunciation:** TEHR-eks

**Meaning:** Latin *terra* (earth) + *rex* (king/ruler). "Ruler of the earth."

**Why it almost works:** Powerful, authoritative, short. OEM-feeling name signals serious equipment technology.

**Why it cannot be used:** Terex Corporation (NYSE: TEX) is a $5B+ global construction equipment manufacturer. The similarity between "TERREX" and "TEREX" would almost certainly invite trademark opposition and litigation. **This name should not be used regardless of other merits.**

---

### #10 — AXIOM

**Pronunciation:** AK-see-um

**Meaning:** From Greek *axios* (worthy) and Latin *axioma* (a self-evident truth). In logic, an axiom requires no proof — it is foundational. "Ground truth" in AI/ML = the verified real-world data that trains models — and the literal ground construction equipment works on.

**Why it works:**
- For conservative infrastructure buyers: "Axiom" says the technology is not a hypothesis, it is a proven fact
- For investors: signals intellectual authority and first-principles thinking
- Shortest, most elegant name on this list as a standalone word

**Domain:** axiom.com is taken (browser automation company). axiom.ai — status unclear, may be dormant. Consider "Axiom Ground" as a compound, or operate as axiom.ai if available.

**Trademark risk:** MEDIUM-HIGH. "Axiom" is used by multiple companies across sectors (Axiom Space, Axiom Data, others). As a standalone mark it would face challenges. As a compound ("Axiom Ground") it is more protectable. Verify carefully before committing.

## Notes on Due Diligence

All domain availability assessments are based on DNS resolution attempts and website content analysis. Before committing to any name:

1. **WHOIS lookup** for both .com and .ai variants
2. **USPTO trademark clearance search** in Class 7 (industrial machinery), Class 9 (AI hardware/software), and Class 42 (technology services)
3. **International trademark search** if EU or APAC markets are near-term targets
4. **Domain acquisition** — even if .com is technically available, check for squatters or price premiums before announcing publicly
5. **Social media handle availability** — check X/Twitter, LinkedIn, YouTube for handle consistency

*Note: Commission a professional trademark attorney for final clearance before committing significant resources to brand development.*
