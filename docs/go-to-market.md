# Go-to-Market: Incubators, Funding & Pitch Strategy

*Research date: March 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

The autonomous construction equipment space has a clear and well-worn funding path, validated by Built Robotics ($112M), SafeAI ($68M, acquired), Bedrock Robotics ($350M, $1.75B valuation), and AIM Intelligent Machines ($50M). The right sequence for a founder with 18 years in ML infrastructure: **Start with Suffolk BOOST + HAX in parallel → build demo and first customer → YC or DCVC seed → SBIR Phase II for non-dilutive capital → Series A at $5M–$15M after 2–3 pilot deployments.** Defense-adjacent funding via SBIR (Army/AFWERX) and DIU can provide non-dilutive capital at each stage. The pitch narrative that has converted investment: **labor shortage + retrofit-first + supervised autonomy wedge + data flywheel.**

## Accelerators & Incubators

### Suffolk Technologies BOOST ⭐ *Highest Priority: Construction-Native*

**What it is:** Investment arm of Suffolk Construction ($5B+ revenue GC), ranked #1 Most Active Construction Tech Investor by AGC 2018–2025. Focused exclusively on the built world (AEC, FM, construction, infrastructure, real estate).

**Terms:** $150K via post-money SAFE for 4.5% ownership. 8-week intensive program. Pro-rata right to invest in next qualified financing round up to 20%.

**Why it's critical:** BOOST gives direct access to active construction job sites for piloting. This is one of the few accelerators that can convert a prototype into a real-world construction deployment within the program. Robotics/equipment applications rose 60% YoY in BOOST 6 (2025) applications.

**Recent relevant cohort (BOOST 6, Sept–Nov 2025):**
- Cyphra Autonomy (autonomous material wagons)
- Puppet Robotics (semi-autonomous welding/abatement arm)
- Dig Energy (geothermal drilling robotics)

**Portfolio:** 38 graduates (Trunk Tools, Exodigo, Rugged Robotics, Kaya AI) — collectively raised $700M+. 54 global partner organizations provide real deployment opportunities.

**Application:** Annual cycle. BOOST 7 applications expected Q1 2026. [suffolktech.com/boost](https://suffolktech.com/boost)

**The pitch here:** Frame the autonomous tractor/excavator as a construction labor solution, not a tech project. Suffolk's operational partners will immediately recognize the labor shortage pain and want to pilot it. Getting a Suffolk-backed GC as your first customer is a major seed fundraising signal.

### HAX (SOSV Hardware Accelerator) ⭐ *Highest Priority: Hardware-Native*

**What it is:** The world's premier hardware startup accelerator, part of SOSV ($1.6B AUM). Newark, NJ headquarters (35,000 sq ft with machine shops, mechanical, chemical, and electronics labs). Also Pune, Tokyo, Shenzhen.

**Terms:** $250K initial ($150K cash + $100K in-kind engineering support), delivered monthly over 6 months. SOSV targets ~10% ownership. Total support up to $550K including in-kind. Follow-on from SOSV funds can reach $4–5M aggregate through Series C/D.

**What they look for:** Validated customer problem, strong engineering team, working prototype. Focus: robotics, advanced manufacturing, climate infrastructure, critical minerals, and industrial independence.

**Track record:** 290+ graduated startups, cumulative value >$9B, nearly $2B raised in follow-on.

**Application:** Rolling admissions, 2–3 companies admitted monthly. 2-week review, 3–4 interview rounds, decision within ~2 weeks of first interview. [hax.co](https://hax.co)

**The pitch here:** Hardware technical depth is HAX's language. The 18-year ML infrastructure background + a working tractor prototype = credible hardware team. Emphasize: retrofit-first approach means faster iteration than building new machines, construction site deployment means a defined private environment, and the SBIR/defense angle shows non-dilutive capital sophistication.

### Y Combinator *High priority; do simultaneously with BOOST/HAX*

**Terms:** $500K for 7% equity (post-money SAFE: $125K for 7% + $375K uncapped). Four batches per year (W, S, F). Three months in SF Bay Area ending in Demo Day. Acceptance: ~1–2% from ~30,000 applicants.

**What they look for:** Complementary technical + business co-founders, demonstrated progress, founder-market fit. For hardware: **a functional prototype is required** — Figma mocks don't work.

**Relevant robotics portfolio:**
- **Flywheel AI** — converts excavators for remote + autonomous operation, trains autonomous policies from construction datasets. **Direct analog to this startup.**
- Tensr (F2025) — fully autonomous robotic factories
- Saphira AI (S2024) — safety certification for industrial robots and heavy machinery
- Gecko Robotics — infrastructure inspection, $125M Series D at $1.2B valuation

**Application:** [ycombinator.com/apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply)

**The pitch here:** Open strong with the labor crisis (92% of contractors can't fill positions, 501K workers short). Show the working prototype video. Emphasize the retrofit moat ("we can deploy on any machine in hours, just like Mobileye vs. building new cars"). The 18-year ML infra background = "technical founder who has shipped at scale." YC loves founders who understand their domain from first principles.

**What it is:** Carnegie Foundry is the startup studio spun out of Carnegie Mellon's National Robotics Engineering Center (NREC). NREC has 856 licensed inventions, developed the autonomy stacks powering Caterpillar and John Deere autonomous equipment. Carnegie Foundry explicitly includes "autonomous construction systems" in its pipeline.

**Engagement options:**

2. **Co-development with NREC** — government grant-funded development; startup retains IP rights to improvements
3. **Carnegie Foundry studio** — pre-formed investment vehicle with dedicated investors

**Why it matters:** A founder with 18 years in ML infrastructure licensing NREC's 856-invention catalog and building on top of it is an extremely compelling story. Non-dilutive path to prototype hardware. Access to the exact researchers who built the world's most successful construction autonomy systems.

**Contact:** cmu.edu/nrec, carnegiefoundry.com

## Defense-Adjacent Funding

### SBIR/STTR *Primary non-dilutive funding path*

The most important non-dilutive funding mechanism. Provides capital to reach each funding stage without dilution.

**DoD SBIR Phase Structure:**
| Phase | Amount | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase I | $50K–$250K | 3–6 months | Feasibility study / proof of concept |
| Phase II | $750K–$3M | 21 months | Prototype development |
| Phase III | Unlimited (sole-source) | Ongoing | Commercialization / government contracts |

**SBIR Phase II ($750K–$3M) provides enough non-dilutive capital to reach TRL 6–7 without giving up equity** — a crucial bridge between pre-seed and seed.

**Relevant solicitation tracks:**
- **Army SBIR:** Monthly open topics targeting AI/ML, autonomy, and sensors. December 2024 topic: "AI/ML-Enabled Voice-Commanded Autonomous Maneuver for Ground Combat Vehicles." Apply: [armysbir.army.mil](https://armysbir.army.mil)
- **AFWERX SBIR:** Paused as of October 1, 2025 (authorization expired); watch for reauthorization. Frame as "autonomous airfield construction and repair" — the Air Force Civil Engineering Center builds airfields worldwide.
- Apply at: [sbir.gov/topics](https://www.sbir.gov/topics) — filter by Army or Air Force, search "construction" and "autonomous ground"

**Dual-use framing:** Autonomous construction equipment for military engineering (road grading, fortification, base construction, rapid airfield damage recovery) is genuinely valuable to the DoD. AIM Intelligent Machines already proved this — their $4.9M Air Force contract for autonomous base construction and RADR.

---

### DIU (Defense Innovation Unit)

**Ground Vehicle Autonomous Pathways (GVAP):** DIU + Army awarded contracts in April 2024 to Forterra, Kodiak Robotics, Neya Systems, Overland AI (navigation); Applied Intuition, Scale AI (ML/autonomy); Anduril, Palantir (software integration).

**How to engage:** DIU posts Commercial Solutions Openings (CSOs) at diu.mil. Apply through "work with us" portal. Key portfolio areas: Autonomy and AI/ML.

**The playbook:** Forterra and Kodiak built commercial autonomous vehicle platforms first, then converted for military use. This is the path — commercial construction first, DoD transition as a revenue amplifier, not the primary business.

---

### NSIN (National Security Innovation Network)

**NSIN Vector:** $25K/$15K/$10K prizes for 1st/2nd/3rd; no equity or IP taken. Focus areas include advanced sensing, autonomy, and command & control. Apply: vector@nsin.mil.

**NSIN Foundry:** 5-month startup studio commercializing DoD lab technologies. $50K prizes. Free to participate. Matches entrepreneurs with DoD-developed IP.

**Why useful:** Low-dollar but zero equity. Good for initial validation and DoD network building.

## The Winning Pitch

### Pitch Architecture (What Has Converted)

Based on the investment narratives for Built Robotics, SafeAI, Bedrock Robotics, Civ Robotics, and AIM Intelligent Machines:

**1. Open with the labor market, not the technology**
Construction is ~500K workers short. 40%+ of the workforce retires in the next decade. **This is not a cool tech pitch — it is a "the world breaks without this" pitch.** Every investor has driven past a half-built building delayed by worker shortages.

**2. Retrofit-first, not new machines**
VCs are allergic to "we build new equipment from scratch." The winner narrative: retrofitting existing fleets eliminates OEM risk, enables faster deployment, and creates an OEM-agnostic moat. Bedrock mounts in hours with no permanent modifications. Built Robotics' Exosystem is an aftermarket kit. SafeAI retrofits any machine.

**3. Supervised autonomy wedge → full autonomy destination**
The commercially viable near-term product: one operator oversees multiple machines remotely/semi-autonomously. Full autonomy is the pitch. Supervised autonomy is the product you deploy this year.

**4. Unit economics at volume**
Show: prototype cost today → target BOM at 100 units → at 1,000 units. Gross margin trajectory to 40%+. Hardware-only companies rarely get funded — the winning story is hardware + software + data flywheel generating recurring revenue.

**5. Real deployments, not lab demos**
- Pre-seed: named pilot partner + unedited video
- Seed: named GC/contractor + quantified outcomes
- Series A: multiple paying customers + $1M+ ARR + clean safety record

**6. Position vs. Komatsu and Caterpillar**
Three working strategies:
- "We become the software layer all OEMs buy or license" (like Mobileye)
- "We partner with OEMs for distribution, own the autonomy stack"
- "We target the retrofit market OEMs won't serve (aging fleet, mixed-brand sites)"

**Never:** "We are better than Komatsu at making autonomous equipment." You can't out-resource a $25B company.

**7. The unfair advantage slide**
"18 years building ML infrastructure and autonomous agent systems that actually work at scale" — this credential does enormous work. Make it explicit and early. Bedrock's "ex-Waymo team" credential compressed their fundraising timeline by years.

## Milestone Gates by Funding Stage

### Pre-Seed ($500K–$2M)

**What you need:**
- Functional prototype on a **real machine** (not simulation) executing one autonomous task reliably in a controlled outdoor environment
- At least one signed pilot agreement or LOI from a GC, equipment rental company, or farm/mine operator
- Clear technical differentiation: why your ML approach works when others have struggled
- Team completeness: hardware co-founder or clear hardware hiring plan

**Realistic investors:** Angels with construction/industrial background, Lemnos Labs, Trucks VC, HAX, BOOST, YC, SBIR Phase I

**Burn rate:** $50K–$150K/month (2–4 person lean hardware team). Target 18 months runway.

---

### Seed ($2M–$5M)

**What you need:**
- Real-world deployment on at minimum one construction site with a **named customer**
- Pilot contract with quantified outcomes (X cubic yards, Y% productivity improvement, Z hours autonomous without incident)
- Initial revenue — even $10K–$50K paid pilot demonstrates willingness to pay
- Defined product: specific use case, specific machine type, specific environment
- SBIR Phase II in hand or awarded ($750K–$3M non-dilutive) materially de-risks the round

**Realistic investors:** Brick & Mortar Ventures, Ironspring Ventures, Builders VC, DCVC, Prime Movers Lab, Lux Capital, HAX follow-on (SOSV)

**Burn rate:** $150K–$350K/month (5–12 person team with hardware prototyping). Target 18–24 months runway.

---

### Series A ($10M–$20M)

**What the business looks like:**
- 3–5 named paying customers (GCs, equipment operators, mines)
- TRL 7–8: system reliably works with multiple customers in real conditions
- $1–3M ARR with consistent MoM growth
- No single customer >30% of revenue
- Gross margins on track toward 40% (software/data recurring revenue on top of hardware)
- Clean safety record (quantified and documented)
- Manufacturing/supply chain plan (contract manufacturer identified or in-house)
- Unit economics modeled through 100-unit and 1,000-unit production scenarios

**Realistic investors:** Lux Capital, DCVC, Prime Movers Lab, General Catalyst, Founders Fund, 8VC, Eclipse, Tiger Global

**Burn rate:** $500K–$1.5M/month. Target 18 months to demonstrable growth metrics.

## Demo Bar at Each Stage

### Pre-Seed Demo Requirements

**Minimum viable:**
- A real machine executing one autonomous task reliably in a controlled outdoor environment
- Demonstrated perception pipeline: machine knows where it is, what's around it, what it's supposed to do
- One complete autonomous cycle without human intervention (e.g., tractor follows field boundary autonomously)
- Safety override clearly demonstrated (E-stop, geofence)

**Format:** Video is acceptable at pre-seed, but must show **full unedited autonomous cycles**, not highlight reels. A 5–10 minute raw video with timestamps is more credible than a polished 90-second cut. Investors in this space have been burned by teleoperated machines presented as autonomous.

**What converts:** Buildroid AI raised $2M pre-seed (Tim Draper) after a live site demo at the Big Five Construction Conference. Sitegeist Robotics raised €4M pre-seed (Feb 2026) after demonstrating on real infrastructure repair sites. Pattern: industry event + real worksite + live demo.

---

### Seed Demo Requirements

**What investors need:**
- Multi-task or multi-environment performance (same machine in multiple conditions, or multiple machines)
- Quantified pilot outcomes: "X cubic yards in Y hours, Z% below human-operator baseline"
- Customer testimonial — having a GC project manager say "this worked on our site" outweighs any technical display

**Format:** Top investors increasingly want a **live site visit**. Salesforce Ventures' robotics investment guide explicitly says: "When possible, see robots in person. Can the robot recover when interfered with? Does it only work in specific environments?" Plan to bring investors to the jobsite.

**What converts:**
- Bedrock Robotics: 130-acre Sundt site, 65K cubic yards excavated before Series A — scale metric was the conversion signal
- Built Robotics: 100,000 tons excavated autonomously before Series B — the cumulative aggregate metric
- Civ Robotics CivDot: 3,000 layout points/day at 8mm accuracy demonstrated in field — precision metric

---

### Series A Demo Requirements

**The bar:**
- Multiple machines running simultaneously or sequentially at a customer site
- Continuous operation data: uptime %, mean time between human interventions, total autonomous hours
- Side-by-side comparison: autonomous machine vs. human operator, measured objectively
- Commercial contract in hand (not just LOI)
- System is software-updatable, centrally monitorable, and doesn't require a robotics PhD at each site

Bedrock's Series A pitch explicitly addressed scalability: "mounts in hours, no permanent modifications, no on-site robotics team required."
